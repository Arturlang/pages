How the pickpocket dice fall

Every lift resolves in two independent rolls: a contested detection check that decides how cleanly it goes, and — for bags — a per-item grab check. Both are driven entirely by the thief's Pickpocketing skill and the mark's stats. Move the dials to see the odds recompute.

thief = roll((skill+1)d6) + ⌊(thiefSPD−10)/3⌋  vs  mark = PER·awareness + ⌊(markSPD−10)/3⌋

What kind of success — the outcome tiers

The same detection check, broken into its six graded bands at the selected Perception. Anything green is a completed steal; the darker the green, the less the mark noticed.

03

The grab check — odds per item

Once a bag is open, each item has its own fine-motor chance: clamp(45 + skill×9 − difficulty, 5, 100). Keys are fiddly; a key on a hip belt is the hardest thing to lift. Keyrings match loose keys. Rings on a finger use their own tighter roll.

04

Reading the numbers

Skill is dice, not a bonus

Pickpocketing 0–6 sets how many d6 you throw: skill+1. A novice throws 1d6 (avg 3.5); a Legendary thief throws 7d6 (avg 24.5). Matthios' boon adds one more die.

Awareness swings everything

An unaware mark counts only PER×0.35. From behind it's the full PER. Facing you, it's PER×1.75 — an alert, sharp-eyed guard is nearly impossible to lift from the front.

Speed is a quiet ±

Each side adds ⌊(SPD−10)/3⌋. Because it floors, being slow hurts a touch more than being fast helps — a fast thief vs a slow mark is worth a few points of margin.

Two rolls, not one

Detection decides if you're noticed; the grab check decides if your fingers close. You can lift cleanly yet fumble the item, or snatch it while setting off alarm — they're independent.
